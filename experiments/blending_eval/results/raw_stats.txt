Blending Strategy A/B Eval - Raw Stats
=======================================

Setup:
  - 5 candidate sources with variable pool sizes [60, 40, 30, 50, 20]
  - 200 total candidates per trial, ~8 real duplicates (15% overlap)
  - 50 trials, fresh seed per trial
  - Each source has a home author cohort of 12 authors (75% probability)
  - fav_count correlated with similarity_score (lognormal boost)
  - similarity_score from Beta(2, 5)
  - Top-K evaluated: 50

Metrics:
  - author_div:       fraction of unique authors in top-50 (higher = diverse)
  - src_entropy:      normalized Shannon entropy of source distribution (1.0 = balanced)
  - src_concentration: max fraction of top-50 from any single source (lower = balanced)
  - mean_relevance:   average similarity_score across top-50 (higher = relevant)
  - top10_relevance:  average similarity_score across top-10 only
  - log_fav_count:    average log(1+fav_count) across top-50 (higher = engaging)
  - recency_hrs:      average hours since creation across top-50 (lower = fresher)

All values: mean ± std over 50 trials.

Key takeaways:
  1. Clean diversity-vs-relevance Pareto frontier across blenders.
  2. SourceTypeBackFill has lowest src_entropy (0.682) by design: backfill
     sources are pushed to the end of the feed.
  3. ContentSignal(fav_count) maximizes engagement (log_fav=7.40) but
     sacrifices 33% relevance vs ContentSignal(similarity).
  4. ContentSignal(recency) produces a 73% fresher feed (22.9h vs ~84h)
     at the cost of 43% relevance.
  5. CountWeighted correctly oversamples larger pools: src_concentration
     0.276 vs 0.208 for plain Interleave.
  6. Switch(freshness-sort) is nearly indistinguishable from Interleave at
     this overlap level (~8 duplicates). Higher overlap would reveal more
     difference.
